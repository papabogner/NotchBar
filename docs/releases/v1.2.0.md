A quick look in your camera, right from the notch.

## What's new

- **Camera glance.** A new camera button sits beside the battery in the open notch. Click it to see yourself in a mirrored live view, so you can check your hair or your background before a call.
- **The camera is only on while you're looking.** It turns off as soon as the notch closes, you pick a tab, or you click the button again. The button turns green while the camera is on. Nothing is recorded or saved.
- **Your Mac's own camera first.** NotchBar uses the built-in camera, so an iPhone nearby isn't woken through Continuity Camera. If the lid is closed, it uses a plugged-in camera instead.
- **Clear messages.** If camera access is turned off, the glance says so and opens the right Settings page in one click. If no camera is free, it says that too.

## Install

You need a MacBook with a notch and macOS 14 or later.

1. Download **NotchBar-v1.2.0.zip** below and open it.
2. Drag **NotchBar** into your **Applications** folder, replacing the old one.
3. Open NotchBar. macOS will say it can't verify the app, because NotchBar isn't notarized by Apple. Click **Done**.
4. Open **System Settings › Privacy & Security**, scroll to **Security**, and click **Open Anyway** next to NotchBar. Confirm with your password.

Your tabs and settings carry over. The first time you click the camera button, macOS asks whether NotchBar may use the camera.

## Good to know

- The app is built by GitHub Actions from this release's source code. To check your download, compare its SHA-256 with the `.sha256` file below: `shasum -a 256 NotchBar-v1.2.0.zip`.
- It isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS treats each update as a new app. You may need to allow it again in **Privacy & Security**, and grant Accessibility again if you use **Replace System HUD**.
